# How AI Changes Policy: Ten Decisions from One Railway Toilet

**Audience:** senior railway management. **Duration:** 40 minutes including live simulator.

1. **The passenger** (3 min): A 45-minute journey to the station and 30-minute wait, yet inadequate WC access. Who owns the failure?
2. **Platform geometry** (4 min): 600-m platform, three distributed blocks, and extra female WCs. Ask managers to change capacity.
3. **Water system** (4 min): GLR, OHT, pumps, STP, equalization and electricity reveal unseen capital and operating constraints.
4. **Service quality** (4 min): Cleaner staffing and response SLA determine whether physical infrastructure is useful.
5. **Coach connection** (4 min): Potential reduction in onboard use and sanitation complaints. Distinguish hypothesis from evidence.
6. **User charges** (4 min): Change a ₹5 tariff and see collection revenue versus discouraged use.
7. **Fare entitlement** (5 min): Annual sanitation full cost / annual boarding. Explain why assigning existing revenue differs from raising fares.
8. **Policy competition** (4 min): Free basic, paid WC, optional premium, low-volume-station subsidy.
9. **AI policy method** (4 min): Question → model → adversarial scenarios → data → pilot → revision.
10. **Executive call** (4 min): Who funds the guaranteed clean-toilet standard and owns evidence and corrective action?

**Message:** AI changes management work by making assumptions inspectable and decisions experimentally testable, not just by producing text.
